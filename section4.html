Section 4 — Factorisation

Quadratic Models and Factorisation

Quadratic models describe situations where the rate of change is itself changing — a ball thrown upward, a company's profit as a function of price, or the area of a rectangle with a fixed perimeter. To look inside these models, we must learn to solve their equations.

Factorisation is the process of rewriting an expression as a product of simpler factors. It is the reverse of expanding, and it serves as our first and most elegant tool for solving quadratic equations cleanly.

Common Factoring & Notable Identities

The first step when factorising is to extract common factors using the distributive property in reverse:

ab + ac = a(b + c)

Recognizable structural shortcuts appear frequently across mathematics:

  • Square of a sum: a2 + 2ab + b2 = (a + b)2
  • Difference of squares: a2 − b2 = (a − b)(a + b)

Demonstration Node

Worked Example 1: Factorise 4x4 + 2x3 − 5x2.

Solution: Extract the common factor x2:
= x2(4x2 + 2x − 5)
