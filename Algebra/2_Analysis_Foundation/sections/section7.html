7. The Product and Quotient Rules

Core Theory: Working with Combined Functions

Imagine you have two different mathematical rules multiplied together, like a length times a width to find an area. If both sides are growing at the same time, you cannot just find their separate derivatives and multiply them!

Why? Because as the shape grows, the changing length stretches across the width, and the changing width stretches across the length. They interact with each other.

✖️ The Product Rule Shortcut
Use this when two functions are multiplied: h(x) = u · v
(u · v)' = u' · v + u · v'
Out loud: "Derivative of the 1st × Leave the 2nd alone + Leave the 1st alone × Derivative of the 2nd."
⚠️ The Trap: Never just multiply individual derivatives! (u · v)' ≠ u' · v'.
➗ The Quotient Rule Shortcut
Use this when functions are divided (fractions): h(x) = uv
u' · v − u · v'v2
Out loud: "Derivative of Numerator × Denominator − Numerator × Derivative of Denominator, all over Denominator squared."
⚠️ The Trap: You must differentiate the numerator first. Order matters for subtraction!

The Proof Explorer (Optional Deep Dive)

For curious minds: here is the step-by-step calculus machinery that proves our shortcuts always work.

📐 Proving the Product Rule (Limit Approach) [Click to toggle]

Let's look at the extra area pieces from our dynamic rectangle on Slide 1 when the side lengths grow by Δu and Δv:

ΔArea = u · Δv + v · Δu + Δu · Δv

To calculate the rate of change, we divide every single piece by the input step change Δx:

ΔAreaΔx = u · ΔvΔx + v · ΔuΔx + Δu · ΔvΔx

Now, we see what happens at the calculus limit as the step shrinks all the way to zero (Δx → 0). The fraction steps transform into exact derivatives (u' and v'), and the leftover change value Δu completely vanishes down to 0:

Area' = u · v' + v · u' + (0 · v') —→ u · v' + v · u'
✨ Because that tiny corner box (ΔuΔv) scales down in two directions at once, it completely disappears at the limit level!
📐 Proving the Quotient Rule (Product Flip Approach) [Click to toggle]

Instead of tracking fractions with limits, we can use algebra to turn our division problem into a simple multiplication problem:

Let our function fraction be defined as f(x) = uv. If we multiply both sides by the bottom value (v), we can rewrite it like this:

u = f · v

Now, let's use the **Product Rule shortcut** we just proved above to differentiate both sides cleanly:

u' = f' · v + f · v'

Our final goal is to isolate the fractional derivative term (f'). Let's swap the positions and substitute f = uv right back into our formula sequence:

f' · v = u' − (uv) · v'

To clean up the fractional subtraction on the right side, we bring them to a common denominator by multiplying u' by vv:

f' · v = u' · v − u · v'v

Finally, divide both sides by the remaining v factor to isolate our final finished identity:

f' = u' · v − u · v'v2

3. Worked Examples

Study these step-by-step applications to master the structural patterns of the Product and Quotient Rules.

Example 7.1: Find the derivative function f'(x):
f(x) = x2 · sin(x)
Solution Steps:
  1. Identify pieces and separate individual terms: u = x2 ⇒ u' = 2x and v = sin(x) ⇒ v' = cos(x)
  2. Assemble into the Product Rule structure u'v + uv':
    (2x) · sin(x) + x2 · cos(x)
  3. f'(x) = 2x·sin(x) + x2·cos(x)
Example 7.2: Differentiate the fractional quotient expression:
g(x) = exx3
Solution Steps:
  1. Identify components: u = ex ⇒ u' = ex and v = x3 ⇒ v' = 3x2
  2. Apply Quotient Rule framework u'v − uv'v2:
    g'(x) = ex(x3) − ex(3x2)(x3)2
  3. Factor out shared expressions from numerator: g'(x) = x2ex(x − 3)x6
  4. g'(x) = ex(x − 3)x4
